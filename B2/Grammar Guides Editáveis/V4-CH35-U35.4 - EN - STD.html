The rule: negative question forms

Put the negative with the auxiliary before the subject. The uncontracted form is formal.

ContractedIsn’t / Don’t / Didn’t + subject
everyday use
Isn’t it late?
UncontractedIs + subject + not?
formal
Is it not late?
Surprise
you did not expect the answer
Don’t you like pizza?
Suggestion or invitation
a friendly suggestion
Why don’t we eat out?
Checking / agreement
you expect a yes
Wasn’t it great?
Criticism
annoyance
Can’t you be quiet?

Surprise or agreement

A negative question often shows surprise or expects you to agree. It is not always a real doubt.

Isn’t it cold?
Don’t you know?
Haven’t you finished?

Answer with yes or no to the facts, not to the question: Don’t you like it? — Yes, I do.

Auxiliary + n’t+Subject+Verb

In formal writing use do you not or is it not.

Pro tip: three quick rules

Contracted order

The contraction stays before the subject.
✓ Aren’t you tired?  ✗ Aren’t tired you?

Uncontracted order

In formal English, not comes after the subject.
Do you not agree?

Answer the fact

Yes = positive. No = negative.
Aren’t you tired? — No, I’m fine.

Watch out!

Are not you coming?
✓ Aren’t you coming? / Are you not coming?

Place not after the subject, or contract it.

Don’t you like pizza? — Yes, I don’t.
✓ Don’t you like pizza? — No, I don’t.

Answer with the real fact.

Why you don’t come?
✓ Why don’t you come?

Use the auxiliary before the subject.
